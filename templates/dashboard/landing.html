About the data

What is this dataset?

Published by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAOSTAT) and shared on Kaggle by Sevgi SY — one of the most comprehensive country-level climate records available publicly.

What it measures
Temperature anomaly — not actual temperature, but how much warmer or cooler a location was compared to the 1951–1980 baseline. A value of +1.2°C means that period was 1.2°C warmer than the 30-year baseline average.
Geographic coverage
284 areas total — 208 sovereign countries, 40 territories, and 36 regional aggregates (Africa, Asia, EU, OECD, Least Developed Countries, etc.). Covers virtually every nation on Earth.
Time coverage
59 years of data from 1961 to 2019, with 17 time periods per area per year — 12 individual months, 4 seasons (DJF, MAM, JJA, SON), and 1 full meteorological year.
Two data elements
Temperature change — the core warming signal used in all visualizations. Standard Deviation — the uncertainty measure for each reading. Together: 569,704 rows in long format.
Why it matters
This is one of the most comprehensive country-level climate records available publicly. It's used to study which countries are warming fastest, how warming varies by season, whether different regions warm in sync, and to build data-grounded projections. The dataset directly supports IPCC findings on global warming acceleration since the 1980s.
Data format
Transformed from wide format (9,656 rows × 66 cols) to long format (569,704 rows × 9 cols) for faster querying. Each row = one area, one month, one year, one value. Browse every row in the Dataset Viewer.
